How Birds Learn to Sing

Most songbirds are not born knowing their song. A young bird listens to adults for weeks before it makes a sound, storing what it hears as a template [1].

Then it starts to babble. The first attempts are quiet and messy, a little like a baby's first words. Dr. Marler called this stage subsong. Over about 3.5 months the bird compares its own voice with the remembered template and corrects itself, note by note.

By its first spring the song is fixed. Some species keep learning all their lives; a mockingbird may know two hundred songs.
