How to pace your first half marathon

Most people who struggle in their first half marathon did not train too little. They ran the first third too fast. This guide is about the one decision that fixes that: what pace to hold, and when.

Pick a goal you can say out loud

Take your best recent 10K time, double it and add ten minutes. That is an honest finish time for a first race.

Start slower than feels right

The first three miles decide the last three. Run them ten to fifteen seconds a mile slower than your goal pace, even if it feels like holding back. Most first-timers do the opposite, and the table below shows what it costs them. A pace band on your wrist helps more than a watch you keep checking.

If you feel good at mile ten, that is the plan working. Speed up then, not before.

  • Eat what you ate before your long runs.
  • Take water at every other station.
  • Walk the stations if you need to. It costs less than you think.

After the line

Keep walking for ten minutes. Eat something within the hour, and leave the next run for two days.

Write down what the last three miles felt like while you still remember. It is the best guide you will have for the next race.
