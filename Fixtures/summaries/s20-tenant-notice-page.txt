Rent increases: what your landlord must do

If you rent your home from a private landlord in Westbrook, there are rules about how and when your rent can go up. This page explains them in plain terms.

How often rent can go up
Your landlord can raise the rent only once in any 12-month period. If you moved in less than 12 months ago, they must wait until 12 months after your tenancy started.

How much notice you must get
Your landlord must give you written notice at least 90 days before the new rent starts. The notice must say the new amount and the date it starts. A text message or a phone call is not enough. An email counts only if your lease says you agreed to receive notices by email.

How much rent can go up
Since January 1, 2026, a rent increase in Westbrook cannot be more than 5% plus the rate of inflation, up to a maximum of 8% in any 12 months. For increases that start in 2027, the maximum is 7.4%. This limit does not apply to buildings that are less than 15 years old or to rooms rented in the landlord's own home.

If you think the increase is too high
You can challenge an increase that breaks these rules. First, write to your landlord and say why you think the increase is not allowed. Keep a copy. If they do not change it within 30 days, you can file a complaint with the Westbrook Rent Board. Filing is free. You must file before the new rent starts, or within 60 days after if you did not get proper notice.

While your complaint is being decided, you should keep paying your old rent. Your landlord cannot evict you for making a complaint.

Getting help
Free advice is available from the Westbrook Tenant Help Line on 555-0177, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Interpreters are available in more than 100 languages. You can also visit the Housing Help Desk at City Hall, Room 104, without an appointment on Tuesdays and Thursdays.
